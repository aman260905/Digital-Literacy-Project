## **Case Study: UPI "Request Money" Fraud**

## **What is UPI "Request Money" Fraud?**


Unified Payments Interface (UPI) fraud involves the exploitation of real-time payment systems to trick users into unauthorized fund transfers. Unlike traditional hacking, this is primarily a social engineering crime that leverages a user’s lack of technical familiarity with the "Request Money" feature.


## **How it Typically Happens (Step-by-Step)**


The Hook: A fraudster poses as a buyer on a secondary marketplace (like OLX) or as a customer care executive. They express urgent interest in a product the victim is selling.

The Deception: The scammer claims they are unable to "send" money and suggests they will send a "payment link" or QR code for the victim to "verify" the transaction.

The Trigger: Instead of a payment, the scammer sends a Collect Request via a UPI app (like Google Pay or PhonePe).

The Mistake: The victim, believing they are receiving money, enters their UPI PIN. They fail to notice that the screen explicitly says "Paying" rather than "Receiving."

The Theft: The moment the PIN is entered, funds are instantly debited from the victim's account and moved to a "mule" account.


## **Target Audience**


While anyone can be a target, scammers frequently eye non-tech-savvy individuals, senior citizens, or small-scale online sellers who are eager to close a sale and may not understand that a PIN is only required for sending money, never for receiving it.


## **Consequences**


The primary consequence is the immediate loss of liquid assets, which is difficult to reverse due to the instantaneous nature of UPI. Victims also face significant emotional distress and a loss of trust in digital banking. In a real-world 2023 incident reported by The Hindu, a Bengaluru resident lost ₹1.5 lakh in minutes after falling for a "fake army officer" scam using this exact "Request Money" tactic.
